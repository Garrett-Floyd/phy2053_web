Theorem 15.2.1. Work-Kinetic Energy Theorem.

The net work done on a system is given by
\begin{equation*} W_{net} = \Delta K \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

We can’t examine the proof for the general theorem because it requires calculus. However, we can prove that theorem is true for work done by a constant net force over a straight line displacement. In this case we have
\begin{equation*} W_{net} = \vec{F}_{net} \cdot \vec{l}, \end{equation*}
and by Newton’s Second Law we have
\begin{equation*} \vec{F}_{net} = m \vec{a}. \end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*} W_{net} = m \vec{a} \cdot \vec{l} = m( a_{x} \Delta x + a_{y} \Delta y + a_{z} \Delta z). \end{equation*}
Since the net force is constant over the displacement the acceleration must be constant also by Newton’s Second Law. Then we can use the equations of kinematices to get rid of the components of acceleration. In particular, consider the following rearrangements of kinematic equations:
\begin{align*} a_{x} \amp = \frac{v_{x,f}^{2} - v_{x,i}^{2}}{2 \Delta x}\\ \\ a_{y} \amp = \frac{v_{y,f}^{2} - v_{y,i}^{2}}{2 \Delta y}\\ \\ a_{z} \amp = \frac{v_{z,f}^{2} - v_{z,i}^{2}}{2 \Delta z}. \end{align*}
Plugging these these in, we get
\begin{align*} W_{net} \amp = m\left[ \left( \frac{v_{x,f}^{2} - v_{x,i}^{2}}{2 \Delta x} \right) \Delta x + \left( \frac{v_{y,f}^{2} - v_{y,i}^{2}}{2 \Delta y} \right) \Delta y + \left( \frac{v_{z,f}^{2} - v_{z,i}^{2}}{2 \Delta z} \right) \Delta z \right]\\ \\ \amp = m \left[ \left( \frac{v_{x,f}^{2} - v_{x,i}^{2}}{2} \right) + \left( \frac{v_{y,f}^{2} - v_{y,i}^{2}}{2} \right) + \left( \frac{v_{z,f}^{2} - v_{z,i}^{2}}{2} \right) \right]\\ \\ \amp = \frac{1}{2}m \left[ \left( v_{x,f}^{2} + v_{y,f}^{2} + v_{z,f}^{2} \right) - \left( v_{x,i}^{2} + v_{y,i}^{2} + v_{z,i}^{2} \right) \right]\\ \\ \amp = \frac{1}{2}m \left( v_{f}^{2} - v_{i}^{2} \right)\\ \\ \amp = \frac{1}{2} m v_{f}^{2} - \frac{1}{2} m v_{i}^{2}\\ \\ \amp = \Delta K \end{align*}
