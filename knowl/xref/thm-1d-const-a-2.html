Theorem 4.1.2.

When the x-component of the acceleration is constant
\begin{equation*} x_{f} = \frac{1}{2} a_{x} \Delta t^{2} + v_{x, i} \Delta t + x_{i}. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

As we proved in Theorem 4.1.1 there is a linear relationship between the x-component of the velocity and \(\Delta t\) if the x-component of the acceleration is zero. For straight lines (\(y = mx + b\)) the average y value is the average of the initial y value and the final y value. In our case
\begin{equation*} v_{avg,x} = \frac{v_{x,i} + v_{x,f}}{2}. \end{equation*}
From Theorem 4.1.1 we have
\begin{equation*} v_{x, f} = a_{x} \Delta t + v_{x, i}. \end{equation*}
Plugging this in to our equation for \(v_{avg,x}\) gives
\begin{align*} v_{avg,x} \amp = \frac{v_{x,i} + a_{x} \Delta t + v_{x,i}}{2}\\ \amp \\ v_{avg,x} \amp = \frac{1}{2} a_x \Delta t + v_{x,i}. \end{align*}
On the other hand, the definition of average velocity ( Definition 3.1.1 ) gives the equation
\begin{equation*} v_{avg,x} = \frac{x_{f} - x_{i}}{\Delta t}. \end{equation*}
Setting these two equations equal to each other, we get
\begin{align*} \frac{x_{f} - x_{i}}{\Delta t} \amp = \frac{1}{2} a_x \Delta t + v_{x,i} \\ \amp \\ x_{f} - x_{i} \amp = \frac{1}{2} a_x \Delta t^{2} + v_{x,i} \Delta t \\ \amp \\ x_{f} \amp = \frac{1}{2} a_x \Delta t^{2} + v_{x,i} \Delta t + x_{i}. \end{align*}
